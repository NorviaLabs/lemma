Why Most Published Research Findings Are False

https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC1182327/fullTextXML

Abstract

There is increasing concern that most current published research findings are false.

Abstract

Simulations show that for most study designs and settings, it is more likely for a research claim to be false than true.

Modeling the Framework for False Positive Findings

Research is not most appropriately represented and summarized by p-values, but, unfortunately, there is a widespread notion that medical research articles should be interpreted based only on p-values.

Section 'It can be proven that most claimed research findings are false'

The probability of a study finding a true relationship reflects the power 1 - β (one minus the Type II error rate). The probability of claiming a relationship when none truly exists reflects the Type I error rate, α.

Modeling the Framework for False Positive Findings

According to the 2 × 2 table, one gets PPV = (1 - β)R/(R - βR + α). A research finding is thus more likely true than false if (1 - β)R > α.

Corollaries, Corollary 1

The smaller the studies conducted in a scientific field, the less likely the research findings are to be true. Small sample size means smaller power and, for all functions above, the PPV for a true research finding decreases as power decreases towards 1 − β = 0.05.

Bias section

Thus, with increasing bias, the chances that a research finding is true diminish considerably.

Corollaries (Corollary 6)

Corollary 6: The hotter a scientific field (with more scientific teams involved), the less likely the research findings are to be true. This seemingly paradoxical corollary follows because, as stated above, the PPV of isolated findings decreases when many teams of investigators are involved in the same field.
