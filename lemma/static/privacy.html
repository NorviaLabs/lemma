Privacy

Last updated 20 October 2026 · OpenLemma at openlemma.org is run by Mohit Ranka · Terms of use

In short

  • OpenLemma keeps your Google name, email address and picture, and the lessons and builds you make.
  • Your AI provider key is never stored. It stays in your browser, which sends it only with each request that needs it.
  • No advertising, no tracking cookies and no request logs. Nothing is sold.
  • OpenLemma's code is public, so you can read exactly what it does with your data.
  • Delete lessons from your library at any time; email contact@openlemma.org to delete your whole account.

What OpenLemma keeps

Your account
When you sign in with Google, OpenLemma keeps your name, email address and profile picture link, and when you joined and last signed in. It asks Google for nothing else.
A session
A cookie keeps you signed in for up to 30 days. The server stores only a fingerprint (hash) of it.
Lessons and builds
Lesson requests, the text of the sources you choose, and build progress are saved so you can leave and come back. Finished lessons are saved to your library automatically, and an interrupted build can be retried. Only your account can open them. Delete stopped builds and lessons from your library to remove them.
Daily usage
How many searches, lesson builds and source reads your account started each day, and how many pieces of feedback it sent, to apply the daily limits. These counts are deleted after seven days.
Feedback
When you send feedback, OpenLemma stores what you wrote (your rating and message), your account's name and email address, the time, and where you were when you sent it: the page, and if relevant the lesson's id and title, an error id, or a build id. We keep it so we can understand and fix problems, improve lessons, and reply to you at that email address. Only the people who run OpenLemma can read it. Each account can send up to 10 pieces of feedback a day. Feedback is kept until we delete it, and it is deleted with your account when you ask us to delete your account.
Anonymous counts
Totals per day of things like lessons opened, goals reached with and without Show me, and builds that passed or failed OpenLemma's checks, to learn whether lessons teach well. Only the day and the kind of event are stored: not who you are, which lesson, or what you searched for.

What OpenLemma doesn't keep

Your AI provider key
It is never saved in OpenLemma's storage: not with your account, your lessons or your builds. Your browser keeps it and sends it with each request that needs it. The server holds it in memory only while that request or build runs, and never writes it to disk. If a provider's error message repeats the key, OpenLemma removes it before saving the build. To retry a build, your browser sends the key again.
Usage logs
The server writes no request logs. When something inside OpenLemma breaks, it records an error id, the time and which part of its code failed, but not who you are, what you asked for, your sources or your key.
Your network address
To stop floods of requests, the server counts recent requests from each network address in memory for up to 10 minutes. The address is never written to disk.

Who else sees your data

Google Cloud
Hosts OpenLemma. Your account, your library, your sources' text and your build requests are stored on a Google Compute Engine server in the United States (Iowa, us-central1).
Google
Handles signing in.
Your AI provider
OpenCode, OpenAI or Anthropic, as you choose, receives your sources' text when you build a lesson, under your own key and their terms.
TypeSafe
Receives, for every search and lesson build:
  • the words you search for, with each result's title and abstract (or, without an abstract, the start of its text), to rank the results by how well they would teach what you searched for;
  • the title and opening text of each source of a lesson you build, to sort the lesson into a subject such as Medicine or Economics;
  • each concept the AI model drafts, with the passages of your sources it is about, to check it for errors before you see it.
It never receives your key or your account. OpenLemma pays for it with the server's TypeSafe key.
Search sources
Wikipedia, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, LibreTexts, OpenAlex, Europe PMC and arXiv receive the words you search for. OpenLemma contacts result sites to check that their full text is readable before recommending them, and fetches a link you paste from its own site.
Cloudflare
Carries traffic to the site, and counts page views with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which uses no cookies and doesn't identify you.

Nothing is sold or shared for advertising.

Deleting your data

Delete any saved lesson from your library at any time. To delete your account and everything in it, email contact@openlemma.org from the address you signed in with.
