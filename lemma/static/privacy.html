Lemma

Privacy

Last updated 17 October 2026. Lemma at tools.mohitranka.com/lemma is run by Mohit Ranka.

What Lemma keeps

  • Your account. When you sign in with Google or GitHub, Lemma keeps your name, email address and profile picture link, which provider you used, and when you joined and last signed in. It asks those providers for nothing else.
  • A session. A cookie keeps you signed in for up to 30 days. The server stores only a fingerprint (hash) of it.
  • Lessons and builds. Lesson requests, selected source text and progress are saved so you can leave and return. Completed lessons are saved automatically. Interrupted builds can be retried. Only your account can open them; delete stopped builds and lessons from your library to remove them.

What Lemma doesn't keep

  • Your AI provider key. It is never saved in Lemma's storage: not with your account, your lessons or your builds. It stays in your browser, which sends it with each request that needs it. The server holds it in memory only while that request or build runs, and never writes it to disk. If a provider's error message repeats the key, Lemma removes it before saving the build. To retry a build, your browser sends the key again.
  • Usage logs. The server writes no request logs, and the site has no analytics or advertising. When something inside Lemma breaks, it records an error id, the time and which part of Lemma's code failed, but not who you are, what you asked for, your paper or your key.

Who else sees your data

  • Your AI provider (OpenCode, OpenAI or Anthropic, as you choose) receives the paper's text when you build a lesson, under your own key and their terms.
  • TypeSafe receives the words you search for with each result's title and abstract (or, without an abstract, the start of its text), to rank the results by how well they would teach what you searched for; and the title and opening text of each source of a lesson you build, to sort the lesson into a subject such as Medicine or Economics. It never receives your key or your account. This happens only where the server has a TypeSafe key, as this hosted Lemma does, and Lemma pays for it with that key.
  • Wikipedia, the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, LibreTexts, OpenAlex, Europe PMC and arXiv receive the words you search for. Lemma contacts result sites to check readable full text before recommending sources. A link you paste is fetched from its own site.
  • Cloudflare carries traffic to the site. Google or GitHub handle signing in.

Nothing is sold or shared for advertising.

Deleting your data

Delete any saved lesson from your library at any time. To delete your account and everything in it, email mohitranka@gmail.com from the address you signed in with.
