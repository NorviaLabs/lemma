Questions and answers

What OpenLemma is, what it costs, and what happens to your key, your sources and your lessons.

In short

  • OpenLemma turns a concept or a source into a short lesson with live models you can move.
  • Look around with no account. Sign in with Google to search for sources and build lessons.
  • Searches and lessons use your own AI provider key, which OpenLemma never stores.
  • Every lesson is checked before you see it, but it can still contain errors.

Getting started

What is OpenLemma?

OpenLemma teaches complex ideas by letting you see them work. Ask about a concept, or bring a paper, article or chapter, and it builds a short, hands-on lesson.

First comes the idea in brief. Then, for each idea, a live model you can move, an explanation with an exact quote from a real source, and a chance to try it yourself.

To see one before you start, open the example lesson or the Gallery.

Is it free?

Yes. OpenLemma is a free learning tool. Searching and building use your own AI provider key: a search asks your provider one short question, to work out what to look up, and your provider may charge for it.

Lessons are built by an AI model using your own key, so your AI provider may charge you for that under its own terms. Those charges are yours.

Do I need an account?

Not to look around. The Gallery and the example lesson work with no account or key.

To search for sources, build lessons and keep a library, sign in with Google. OpenLemma keeps your name, email address and picture, and asks Google for nothing else.

Is OpenLemma open source? Can I run it myself?

Yes. OpenLemma is open source under the MIT License, and the code is at github.com/NorviaLabs/lemma.

Your own copy works the same as openlemma.org. You sign in with your own Google sign-in client and build with your own AI provider key. The README explains the setup.

How long does a lesson take to build?

First, OpenLemma reads your sources and proposes an outline of a few concepts, usually up to eight. You can reorder the concepts, remove any, or mark ones you already know.

Building then takes a few minutes, and you can start the first concept before it finishes. Leaving the page doesn't stop the build. The finished lesson is saved to your library.

Your key and your data

Why do I need my own AI provider key, and which providers work?

Lessons are written by a large AI model of your choice, under your own account with that provider. You connect it once, on your account page.

  • OpenCode Go, OpenAI or Claude, each with an API key from that provider.
  • ChatGPT Plus or Pro, signed in with no key, but only when OpenLemma runs on your own computer. It isn't offered at openlemma.org.

Long sources fit best with OpenCode Go or Claude, which can read about a million tokens (roughly 750,000 words) at once.

Is my key safe?

Your key is never saved in OpenLemma's storage: not with your account, your lessons or your builds. Your browser keeps it and sends it only with each request that needs it.

The server holds the key in memory only while that request or build runs, and never writes it to disk. If a provider's error message repeats the key, OpenLemma removes it before saving the build.

The model is called with no tools, so it can only read what OpenLemma sends and reply with text.

Who else sees my data?

Lessons in your library are private to your account. A few services see parts of what you do, only to make OpenLemma work:

  • Your AI provider receives your question and your level when you search, to work out what to look up, and your sources' text when you build a lesson, under your own key.
  • TypeSafe receives your search words and results, the opening of each source, and each drafted concept, to rank results, sort lessons into subjects and check concepts for errors. It never receives your key or your account.
  • Search sources such as Wikipedia, OpenAlex and arXiv receive the words you search for, and Wikipedia the article titles your AI provider suggests.
  • Google hosts OpenLemma and handles signing in. Cloudflare carries traffic and counts page views without cookies.

Feedback you send, with your name and email address, is read only by the people who run OpenLemma.

OpenLemma keeps no request logs and no tracking cookies, and nothing is sold or shared for advertising. The Privacy page has every detail.

Sources and lessons

What can I bring as sources, and how many?

Ask about a concept and OpenLemma suggests sources to teach from, such as encyclopedia articles, textbooks and open papers. You can change the selection.

Or bring your own: links (an arXiv link becomes its PDF), PDFs, saved web pages and text files.

  • One lesson combines up to 10 links and files, and up to 5 of them can be files.
  • PDFs can be up to 40 MB. Other files can be up to 10 MB.
  • Sources need text that can be selected. Scanned PDFs are refused.
  • Everything must fit in what your chosen model can read at once.

Only bring sources you're allowed to use.

Can a lesson be wrong?

Yes. Lessons are generated by AI and can contain errors, even after OpenLemma's checks. Check what matters against the sources, and don't rely on a lesson for medical, legal, financial or other professional decisions.

Before you see a lesson, OpenLemma checks that every quote appears in its source, every model gives real numbers across its whole range, and every goal can be reached. A lesson that fails is never shown. A concept that can't be fixed is left out, and the lesson says so.

Each model is labelled as a simplified teaching model, with a note on whether its numbers are only illustrative.

How are quotes checked?

Every concept quotes a source word for word. OpenLemma checks that each quote really appears in that source, ignoring only the spacing, hyphens and punctuation that reading a PDF can scramble. Each explanation names the source it comes from, so you can look it up.

Each drafted concept is also checked by TypeSafe's checking model against the passages it's about, for claims the source doesn't support, invented findings and wrong answers.

Can I download a lesson and use it offline?

Yes. Download any lesson as a single file. It opens in a browser with no internet connection, makes no network requests and sends nothing anywhere.

A lesson you download is yours to use, subject to the rights in the sources it quotes.

Can I change a lesson?

Yes, you can edit the lessons in your library. Every save runs the same checks again, so a broken lesson can't be saved or downloaded.

Limits and your account

What are the daily limits?

Each account on openlemma.org can, every day:

  • search for sources 40 times,
  • start 20 outlines or lesson builds,
  • read 100 sources.

The limits reset at midnight UTC, and at most two builds can run at once. They exist because OpenLemma pays for some of the checks behind every search and build.

How do I delete my lessons or my account?

Delete any lesson, or a stopped build, from your library at any time.

To delete your whole account and everything in it, email contact@openlemma.org from the address you signed in with.

How do I report a problem or contact you?

Email contact@openlemma.org: to report a mistake in a lesson, a lesson or source that infringes your rights, misuse of the site, or anything else.

If something broke, the error message shows an id. Include it, so the failure can be found.

When you're signed in, you can also choose Send feedback in the footer, or Report this under an error that shows an id or a build that stopped with an error. Your message, your name and email address, and where you were go to the people who run OpenLemma, so they can reply.
